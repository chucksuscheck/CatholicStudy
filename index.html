Learning by heart

Words you know by heart go everywhere with you: at Mass, at a bedside, in the car, in the middle of the night. You don’t need a book or a screen to pray them, and in time they stop being something you recite and become something you mean.

Memorizing isn’t a talent. It’s a handful of habits, done a few minutes at a time. Every practice page on this site walks you through the same five steps.

The five steps

  1. 1StudyRead the text in its movements, the few short sections it falls into, each with a name and a hook. Learn the shape first; the words hang on it.
  2. 2FadeRead it aloud while more and more of the words disappear, until you’re supplying most of them yourself.
  3. 3InitialsOnly the first letter of each word is left. Say each line from those letters alone.
  4. 4Next lineYou’re shown one line and say the line that follows. This is how you’ll actually remember it: one line leading to the next.
  5. 5ReciteSay the whole thing from memory, then check yourself against the text.

What helps

Say it out loud

Words you speak are remembered better than words you read silently. Whisper if you have to, but use your voice.

A little, often

Ten minutes on each of several days does more than an hour in one sitting. Stop while it still feels easy.

Recall, don’t reread

Trying to bring the words back, even when it’s hard, is what makes them stick. Rereading only feels like progress.

Sleep on it

Memory settles overnight. If a line won’t come today, it often will tomorrow.

Come back to review

Once you’ve learned something, recite it again a few days later, then a week later. Each review makes it last longer.

Pray it, don’t just say it

Think about what the words mean as you say them. Meaning is the strongest hook of all.
